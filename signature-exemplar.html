Diagnoses Taught at Length

DiagnosisVital SignsHallmark SymptomsPriority Problems
DIC
  • ↓ BP, SpO2.
  • Bleeding: oozing from insertion sites, petechiae, ecchymosis.
  • Microclots: organ ischemia, skin signs on the toe tips, severe pain.
  • Bleeding.
  • Ineffective peripheral tissue perfusion.
The bleeding patient and anemia
  • ↑ HR, RR.
  • ↓ BP.
  • Hypoxia: fatigue, SOB, angina, intermittent claudication, headache.
  • Low volume: pale, cool skin, low urine output.
  • Deficient fluid volume.
  • Activity intolerance.
Acute pancreatitis
  • ↑ HR, temp.
  • ↓ BP.
  • Pancreas digesting itself: epigastric pain radiating to the back, worse supine, guarding, distension.
  • Fluid loss: heavy nausea and vomiting, quick dehydration.
  • Severe: Cullen's sign, Grey Turner's sign.
  • Deficient fluid volume.
  • Unstable blood glucose.
Acute coronary syndrome (ACS)
  • ↑ HR, RR.
  • Myocardial ischemia: chest pressure not relieved by rest or nitrates, nausea, diaphoresis, dyspnea.
  • Ischemic conducting cells: dysrhythmias.
  • Decreased cardiac tissue perfusion.
Acute heart failure
  • ↑ HR, RR.
  • ↓ BP, SpO2.
  • Backup into the lungs: SOB, crackles, blood-tinged sputum, orthopnea.
  • Backup into the veins: JVD, pitting edema, ascites, hepatomegaly.
  • Low cardiac output: fatigue.
  • Impaired gas exchange.
  • Excess fluid volume.
Pre-renal AKI
  • ↑ HR.
  • ↓ BP.
  • Kidneys underperfused: oliguria, about 1 day after a hypotensive event.
  • Deficient fluid volume.
  • Electrolyte imbalance: a high potassium.
GI bleed, including variceal
  • ↑ HR, RR.
  • ↓ BP, SpO2.
  • Blood in the GI tract: bright red emesis or stool if active, melena if older.
  • Low volume: pale, cool, clammy, falling urine output.
  • Low hemoglobin: weakness, SOB, angina, syncope.
  • Deficient fluid volume.
  • Aspiration, in a variceal bleed.
Hepatic failure
  • ↑ Temp.
  • Ammonia: decreased LOC, irritability, asterixis, convulsions.
  • Too few clotting factors: bruising, bleeding, varices.
  • Low albumin: ascites.
  • Acute confusion.
  • Bleeding.
TBI with increased ICP
  • ↑ Temp, pulse pressure.
  • ↓ HR.
  • Increased ICP, awake: severe headache, vomiting, blurred vision, lethargy.
  • Pressure on the brain: falling GCS, unequal pupils.
  • Late: Cushing's triad.
  • Ineffective cerebral tissue perfusion.
  • Ineffective airway clearance.
Stroke
  • ↑ BP, when hemorrhagic.
  • Lost blood flow to one side of the brain: sudden one-sided weakness or numbness, slurred speech, visual disturbance.
  • Sudden severe headache: "worst headache of my life".
  • Ineffective cerebral tissue perfusion.
  • Aspiration.
Spinal cord injury
  • ↓ BP, HR, temp.
  • Spinal shock: no movement, sensation or reflexes below the injury.
  • Diaphragm affected, cervical or thoracic: impaired breathing.
  • Ineffective breathing pattern.
  • Ineffective tissue perfusion: the spinal cord.

Diagnoses Taught Briefly

DiagnosisVital SignsHallmark SymptomsPriority Problems
Thrombocytopenia and HIT
  • ↑ Temp, when chemo or the bone marrow is the cause.
  • Too few platelets: petechiae, purpura, ecchymosis, oozing mucous membranes, bleeding in stool and urine.
  • Bleeding.
The immunocompromised patient
  • ↑ Temp.
  • No white cell response: no symptoms until an infection.
  • Opportunistic pneumonia: chills, cough, dyspnea, fatigue.
  • Infection.
Aortic aneurysm
  • ↑ HR, when ruptured.
  • ↓ BP, when ruptured.
  • Intact: usually none. Sometimes back or abdominal pain.
  • Ruptured, bleeding out: chest pain, diaphoresis.
  • Deficient fluid volume.
  • Ineffective tissue perfusion.
Cardiac tamponade
  • ↑ HR.
  • ↓ BP.
  • Blood compressing the heart: Beck's triad, low BP, JVD, muffled heart sounds.
  • After surgery: chest tube output stops abruptly.
  • Decreased cardiac output.
Pericarditis
  • ↑ HR, temp.
  • Inflamed pericardium: friction rub, pain that mimics an MI, dyspnea.
  • Acute pain.
  • Decreased cardiac output.
Valve disease and infective endocarditis
  • ↑ Temp, in endocarditis.
  • ↓ BP.
  • Blood backing up behind the valve: exertional dyspnea, fatigue, chest pain, a murmur.
  • Endocarditis infection: chills, diaphoresis.
  • Decreased cardiac output.
  • Hyperthermia, in endocarditis.
AV blocks
  • ↓ HR.
  • Rate too slow: compromised cardiac output, more symptomatic with each degree.
  • Decreased cardiac output.
Ischemic bowel
  • ↑ Temp.
  • Bowel wall dying: abdominal pain after eating, urgent loose stools, bloody stool with dark red clots.
  • Ineffective GI tissue perfusion.
  • Deficient fluid volume.
Bowel obstruction and paralytic ileus
  • ↑ HR.
  • Peristalsis blocked or lost: distension, cramping pain in waves, bilious vomiting, no flatus.
  • Bowel sounds: high-pitched and tinkling in an SBO, absent to hypoactive in an ileus.
  • Dysfunctional GI motility.
  • Deficient fluid volume.
Abdominal compartment syndrome
  • ↑ HR, RR.
  • ↓ BP.
  • Fluid pressing on the organs: hugely distended, possibly rigid, very painful.
  • Ineffective tissue perfusion.
Meningitis
  • ↑ Temp.
  • Inflamed meninges: throbbing headache, nuchal rigidity, vomiting.
  • Hyperthermia.
  • Ineffective cerebral tissue perfusion.
Seizures
  • ↑ HR, BP, temp.
  • ↓ SpO2.
  • Brain injury irritating the tissue: decreased neurologic status with no clear cause.
  • Emergency: status epilepticus.
  • Injury.
  • Ineffective airway clearance.
