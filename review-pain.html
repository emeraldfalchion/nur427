Pain Management

Patient-Controlled Analgesia (PCA)

For further study, see Fundamentals II Week 6 – Pain.

SettingWhat It Does
Loading dose
  • Usually given at the start, though not required. Gets the pain under control, then the PCA takes over.
PCA dose (bolus)
  • An extra dose each time the patient pushes the button.
Lockout interval
  • How often the patient can get a PCA dose.
  • The button lights up when the patient can push it, and stays unlit during the lockout.
Maximum limit
  • The most the patient can get in 4 hours.
Continuous (basal) dose
  • Runs whether or not the patient pushes the button.
  • Example: one hospital's protocol gives none to most patients, and a continuous dose with a pulse ox to opioid-tolerant ones.

Nursing Responsibilities for a PCA

  • Program the pump with the provider's orders, or the pain team does. A PCA is always IV.
  • A naloxone order is a must for every patient with a PCA.
  • Pulse ox on almost every PCA patient.
  • Y tubing: the PCA joins a carrier IV fluid, usually 0.9% normal saline at a low rate, to push its small doses in.
Only the patient pushes the button. Teach the patient and the family. A family member who pushes it for the patient's pain can cause respiratory depression.
