Acute Pancreatitis

Causes and Pathophysiology

Acute inflammatory disease of the pancreas. Most common causes: alcohol or biliary disease.

Causes of acute pancreatitis: I GET SMASHED.
  • I: Idiopathic.
  • GET: Gallstones, Ethanol (alcohol), Trauma.
  • SMASHED: Steroid use, Mumps, Autoimmune, Scorpion stings, Hypercalcemia and hypertriglyceridemia, Endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography (ERCP), Drugs and medications.

How Acute Pancreatitis Occurs

Enzymes Leak

Pancreatic enzymes leak from inside the pancreas into other pancreatic tissue.

Autodigestion

The pancreas starts digesting itself, and its cells necrotize.

Inflammatory Mediators Released

Dying cells set off the inflammatory response and the complement cascade. Protective, but here it can make things worse.

Systemic Inflammatory State

Mediators in the bloodstream injure the vessels and further organ systems, usually the liver and kidneys. Starts localized, then can accelerate to systemic.

Signs and Symptoms

Signs and Symptoms of Acute Pancreatitis

  • Pain: epigastric, mild to severe. Can radiate to the flank, the back or both. Typically constant, dull and aching, and worse lying supine.
  • Abdominal guarding and distension.
  • Cullen's sign and Grey Turner's sign.
  • Nausea and vomiting: heavy.
  • Dehydration: quick, because appetite is lost.
  • Fever, tachycardia, jaundice.
  • Restlessness.

Severity

Mild

  • Most cases. Self-limiting: the patient self-corrects.
  • Mild abdominal pain, with elevated amylase and lipase.
  • Treatment: bowel rest, IV hydration and pain management.

Severe

  • What typically leads to critical care.
  • Requires: close hemodynamic monitoring (blood pressure, heart rate, oxygen), nutritional support, infection control and pharmacologic support.
  • High risk for dysfunction of other organs. See Systemic Complications below.

Labs

LabWhat to Know
LipaseMost diagnostic. Most indicative of pancreatitis and of its severity. Trended.
AmylaseNot as specific to the pancreas as lipase. Both are used, and both are trended.
AlbuminTrended to look at fluid volume status.
ElectrolytesPotassium, magnesium and calcium. Hypocalcemia can point to severe pancreatitis. A low calcium within 48 hours is a cue that the patient is at very high risk for severe problems.
CRPAn inflammatory marker that may also be checked.

Treatment of Acute Pancreatitis

FocusWhat to Know
Maintain respiratory function
  • Acute pancreatitis is one of the biggest risk factors for ARDS, because of the systemic inflammatory response.
Fluid replacement
  • Huge risk for hypovolemic shock. Fluid collects in the retroperitoneal space and cavity, and can sequester as much as one third of the plasma volume.
  • Shock and hypovolemia are the primary cause of death.
  • Usually replaced with colloids, such as albumin, which pull volume back into the vascular system. LR replaces volume as well.
Electrolyte replacement
  • Potassium, calcium and magnesium: monitored, and replaced as needed.
Glucose
  • Can cause hyperglycemia. Pancreatitis that is severe enough can cause hypoglycemia instead.
  • Close monitoring. An insulin drip or a dextrose drip may be needed, depending on severity.
Pain management
  • Quite a bit is needed. Keep a close eye on it.
Abdominal compartment syndrome
  • High risk, because fluid shifts into the abdominal compartment.
  • Treated with surgical decompression.
DIC and hemorrhage
  • High risk for both.
  • Hemorrhage: especially in hemorrhagic pancreatitis. Necrotizing pancreatitis or a pseudocyst can also develop.

Minimize Pancreatic Stimulation

Rest the GI Tract

  • For every case, mild or severe.
  • Why: the pancreas senses eating and releases its enzymes, such as trypsin, to digest the food. Rest turns that off, so no food or drink (NPO).
  • Duration: depends on the patient. Usually until hemodynamically stable, with pancreatic enzymes trending down.
  • Nutrition: severe cases usually need TPN.
  • NG tube placement may be needed.
  • Drug therapy, often needed: antacids, PPIs, anticholinergics.

Systemic Complications

Severe acute pancreatitis can affect every organ system. Assess astutely, trend the findings, and keep a close eye on fluid status and electrolyte status.
ComplicationWhat to Know
PulmonaryHypoxemia, insufficiency or failure, pleural effusions, ARDS. A really big impact on oxygenation.
CardiovascularPericardial effusion, from the fluid volume shifting.
Hypovolemic shockFrom the fluid shifting. See Fluid Replacement above.
SepsisHigher risk of infection with a necrotizing, hemorrhagic pancreas. Can look like peritonitis or sepsis.
RenalAcute kidney injury, from the fluid shifting.
NeurologicDecreased LOC. Severe cases usually end up paralyzed and sedated on a ventilator until the pancreas settles.
HematologicDisseminated intravascular coagulation (DIC).
MetabolicHyperglycemia. See Glucose above.

Prevent and Treat Complications

MeasureWhat It Involves
Early recognition and treatment
  • With known severe acute pancreatitis, trend the respiratory assessments and all the electrolytes.
Close multi-system monitoring
  • Urine output, liver enzymes and the respiratory system: high risk for AKI and ARDS.
  • Electrolytes, glucose, insulin.
  • Pulmonary hygiene, at a high level.
  • Cardiac monitoring.
  • Antibiotic therapy.
Drainage of abscess material
  • CT-guided drain placement.
  • Fluid culture and gram stain.
Debridement of the pancreas
  • Surgical.
  • Necrotic tissue, abscess and fluid collections.
Partial pancreas resection
  • May be needed, in addition to resting the gut.
